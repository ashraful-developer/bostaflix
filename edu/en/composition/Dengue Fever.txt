Dengue fever is a serious mosquito-borne viral disease. It is common in tropical and subtropical countries, including Bangladesh. In recent years, dengue has become a major public health concern in our country. Every year, many people are infected with this disease, and some of them die from its complications. Therefore, everyone should know about dengue, its causes, symptoms, prevention, and treatment.

Dengue fever is caused by the dengue virus. The virus is mainly transmitted by the bite of an infected female *Aedes* mosquito. The *Aedes* mosquito is usually active during the daytime, especially in the early morning and before sunset. It can breed in small amounts of stagnant water. Flowerpots, discarded bottles, cans, coconut shells, buckets, tires, drains, and other containers can become breeding places for these mosquitoes.

The symptoms of dengue usually appear a few days after an infected mosquito bites a person. The most common symptom is a sudden high fever. Other symptoms include severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain, weakness, nausea, vomiting, and skin rashes. Some patients may also experience loss of appetite. In serious cases, dengue can cause bleeding, a sharp fall in blood pressure, shock, and damage to important organs. Such cases require immediate medical attention.

Dengue spreads rapidly when there are many breeding places for *Aedes* mosquitoes. During the rainy season, water often accumulates in different places around houses, schools, offices, and roads. If this water remains stagnant, mosquitoes can easily breed there. Poor waste management and careless disposal of containers also increase the risk of dengue. Rapid urbanization and densely populated areas can make the situation worse.

The best way to control dengue is to prevent mosquito breeding. We should not allow water to remain stagnant around our homes. Water containers should be covered properly and cleaned regularly. Flowerpot trays, buckets, tires, cans, bottles, and other containers should be emptied or removed. Roofs, drains, gardens, and construction sites should also be kept clean. People should use mosquito nets, screens, and mosquito repellents when necessary. Wearing clothes that cover the arms and legs can also reduce mosquito bites.

If someone develops symptoms of dengue, he or she should take adequate rest and drink plenty of safe fluids. The patient should consult a doctor, especially if the fever is high or symptoms become worse. Self-medication should be avoided. Certain medicines, particularly aspirin and some other painkillers, can increase the risk of bleeding in dengue patients. Therefore, medicines should be taken only according to medical advice. Warning signs such as severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, extreme weakness, difficulty breathing, or unusual sleepiness require urgent medical care.

The government and local authorities also have an important role in controlling dengue. They should regularly clean drains, remove stagnant water, manage waste properly, and conduct mosquito-control programmes. Public awareness campaigns through television, radio, newspapers, schools, and social media can help people understand how to prevent dengue. Schools can also teach students to keep their surroundings clean and free from mosquito breeding sites.

In conclusion, dengue fever is a dangerous but largely preventable disease. It is not enough to depend only on hospitals or government mosquito-control programmes. Every individual, family, school, and community must work together to reduce mosquito breeding. If we keep our surroundings clean, remove stagnant water, protect ourselves from mosquito bites, and seek proper medical care when necessary, we can greatly reduce the spread of dengue. Awareness, cleanliness, and collective action are the best weapons against dengue fever.
